veriT proofs Carcara rejected over int/real subtyping
======================================================

653 of the 23,328 veriT benchmarks in the round-four SMT-LIB run failed with

    sort error: expected 'Real', got 'Int'

from Carcara's parser, before any checking began. 650 of them check *valid*
after the fix (round six); the remaining 3 are listed separately at the end and
fail for unrelated reasons.


What veriT emits
------------------------------------------------------

One rule, one operator, one shape. Every rejected position is the `=` of a
`unary_minus_simplify` step:

    (! (- 1.0) :named @p_1)                                   in an assumption
    ...
    (step t28 (cl (= @p_1 (- 1))) :rule unary_minus_simplify)

`@p_1` names `(- 1.0)`, which is Real. veriT prints the simplified right-hand
side as `(- 1)` -- unary minus on an *integer* literal, so Int. The `=` therefore
has Real on one side and Int on the other. `(- 1)` itself is fine: `-` is one of
the operators whose argument sorts are fixed and numeric, and those already
honoured `--allow-int-real-subtyping`. It is the `=` above it that was rejected.

Measured on a stratified sample of 36 proofs, six per family across all nine
families, regenerated with the runner's own veriT invocation:

  - the position Carcara reported is the `=` of a `unary_minus_simplify` step in
    36 of 36. No other operator and no other rule appears.
  - the parser stops at its first error, so that alone would only characterise
    the *first* offending term. Scanning every step of those 36 proofs for the
    same shape -- an integer literal equated with a named real-sorted term --
    finds 221 occurrences, again all `unary_minus_simplify` and no other rule.

So on this population the defect is reached through exactly one of the three
positions the fix relaxed.


What Carcara fixed (commit 92d8a42f, `parser: honour Int/Real subtyping at the
polymorphic positions`)
------------------------------------------------------

`--allow-int-real-subtyping` was consulted for the operators whose argument sorts
are fixed and numeric, but not for the positions where the sort is not fixed by
the operator and is merely required to *agree*. Three call sites changed, from
`check_sort_eq`/`check_sort_all_eq` to the `_poly` variants:

    arguments of `=` and `distinct`     <-- the one these proofs trip on
    the two branches of an `ite`
    arguments of an uninterpreted function

Already correct, because their argument sorts are fixed and numeric:

    + - * / abs, and the order comparisons < <= > >=

Deliberately still strict, because they require a *specific* numeric sort:

    div mod to_int is_int, and the trigonometric operators

so `(div 1.0 2.0)` is still rejected, and nothing changes when the flag is off.

The same defect had appeared in an earlier three-logic round on 194 QF_LRA
proofs, where it was misattributed to lia_generic residue.
